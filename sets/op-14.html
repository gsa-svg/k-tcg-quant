Buggy on top, and the run starts quoting itself

The Azure Sea's Seven produced the fandom's favorite market fact: Buggy — the clown — leads the chase table, in gold-stamped and standard SP forms. Behind the joke is a structural shift worth noticing: much of this set's top ten consists of stamped reprints of older hits, cards carrying OP-09 and PRB-02 numbers. The run has started quoting its own back catalogue.

Stamped reprints behave differently from originals — they add supply to a character while remaining distinct collectibles, and their prices key off the original printing. A box this recent is still settling, so treat the live table above as the current read rather than a settled ranking.

OP-14 box price: the six-month trajectory

The Japanese sealed box entered Jan 2026 around $63 and stands near $88 as of 2026-07-12 — it gained 40% over the window, peaking at $93 in the week of 2026-05-13. The English-language OP-14 box trades near $246 over the same period (+4% since January) — about 2.8x the Japanese box, a gap driven by print volume and Western demand rather than card content.

Is an OP-14 booster box worth buying? (Aug 2026)

The box sits near its tracked high ($88 vs peak $93, +40% over our window) — the market has rewarded holders, and a buyer today is paying for that momentum to continue. The chase math is lottery-shaped: the top card alone (Buggy OP09 051 SP Gold) is worth about 14x the box, so a box's expected value concentrates in a few low-odds hits.
