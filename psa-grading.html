How to read this

Total graded is every card from that set sitting in a PSA holder, at any grade — not one specific card. Gem is the share that came back PSA 10. Week is how many new grades appeared between 2026-07-22 and 2026-07-29, and % is that change against the earlier total.

We never add the two editions together. Japanese and English are separate print runs with different card stock and different print quality, so a combined gem rate would describe neither. 2 sets have no English row at all — those printings have not been released, which is different from zero cards graded.

A high weekly number is not automatically bullish. It means packs are being opened, which thins the sealed supply, but it also means more graded copies competing on the market. Read it next to the box price on each set guide, and against completed sales on the PSA 10 value ranking.

Population figures are compiled from public PSA population reporting. We publish weekly change only from the point we began recording it ourselves; we do not republish historical series compiled by others.

What the population data shows right now

OP-13 Carrying on His Will carries the largest graded population of any tracked set — 43,558 Japanese cards in PSA holders, plus 83,169 English. That is what years of opening does: the set has been cracked, submitted and re-submitted since 2022, and its population still grows every week. Newer sets sit at a fraction of that and climb faster in percentage terms precisely because their base is small.

The fastest-growing Japanese population this week is OP-16 at +55.23% (+317 cards), followed by OP-15 at +13.28%. Fresh sets spike right after release as chase cards go straight from pack to grading queue; a mature set that suddenly accelerates usually means a price move made grading worth the fee again.

Gem rates are not uniform. Among Japanese printings, OP-06 currently gems at 94% while OP-11 sits at 83.2%. The spread reflects print quality and card-stock differences between production runs — and it is why a PSA 10 from a low-gem set commands a wider premium over raw than one from a set where nine in ten submissions gem.

For 8 sets (OP-13, OP-11, OP-06, OP-12, OP-08, PRB-02, OP-10, EB-02) the English graded population is actually larger than the Japanese one — a reminder that the two markets have different collector bases, and another reason we never merge the columns.

PSA is one of three graders we track. CGC and TAG populations — including CGC's split between Pristine 10 and Gem Mint 10, and TAG's 10 versus 10P — are shown per set on each set guide, always kept separate from PSA because the standards do not map onto each other.

PSA population — common questions

Which One Piece set has the most PSA-graded cards?

OP-13 Carrying on His Will, with 43,558 Japanese cards graded and 83,169 English as of 2026-08-02. See the table above for every tracked set.

Why does grading volume matter for sealed box prices?

Every graded card came out of an opened pack. When a set's population climbs quickly, sealed boxes of that set are being destroyed at pace — which is supply pressure on the box market. We read population growth alongside the box price series on each set guide.

Why don't you add Japanese and English together?

They are different print runs with different card stock, pull rates and buyers. A combined total, or worse a combined gem rate, would describe neither market. Every figure on this site is labelled by printing.

Where does this data come from?

Public PSA population reporting, collected by us weekly and appended to a ledger we never rewrite. Weekly change is only shown for the period we recorded ourselves — we do not backfill history we did not observe.
